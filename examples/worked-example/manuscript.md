# Evidential Sufficiency for Causal Claims about Intervention X in Older Adults

## Executive summary

The available evidence supports a **bounded association** between intervention X
and improved outcomes among adults aged 65-74 in a single urban cohort. It does
**not** license a causal claim, and it does not license generalisation to older
adults as a population. An experimental or quasi-experimental design in a broader
population is required before a causal claim can be made.

## Finding

Among participants aged 65-74 in a single urban cohort, the intervention group
showed improvement on the primary outcome relative to controls (source: [synthetic]
Cross-sectional study of intervention X in an urban cohort, 2024, p. 6, Table 2).
This association is well supported and is not disputed here.

## Why this does not support a causal claim

The primary study used a cross-sectional observational design (ibid., p. 4,
Methods). A cross-sectional observational design cannot distinguish causation from
association, and therefore cannot discharge a causal claim. This is the standard
design-licensing principle in psychological methodology, and it is not a
technicality: the same data are consistent with the intervention producing the
outcome, the outcome producing uptake of the intervention, and a third factor
producing both.

A pre-registered replication in a rural cohort did not reproduce the association
(source: [synthetic] Pre-registered replication in a rural cohort, 2025, p. 11).

## The strongest objection, and our response

**Objection.** The replication failure may reflect population differences between
urban and rural cohorts rather than absence of effect. If so, the effect could be
real but bounded.

**Response.** Conceded. This is a live possibility and is retained as a rival
reading. It does not, however, restore the causal licence. "Real but
population-bounded" and "no effect" are both consistent with the present evidence,
and no design in the evidence base distinguishes them.

## Scope of this assessment

This assessment concerns **causal licensing only**. It does not claim the
intervention is ineffective. Absence of a licensed causal claim is not evidence of
absence.

## What would change this conclusion

An experimental or quasi-experimental design, adequately powered, in a population
broader than a single urban cohort, with a validated measure of the named
construct.
