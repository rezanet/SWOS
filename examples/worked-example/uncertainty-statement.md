# Uncertainty Statement

Typed uncertainty. Each type carries a different required response.

| Type | Where | Response taken |
|---|---|---|
| `conflicting_evidence` | Urban cohort association vs rural replication failure | Both presented. Contradiction recorded. **No winner adjudicated for narrative tidiness.** |
| `method_uncertainty` | Cross-sectional design in the primary source | Causal licence withheld; claim bounded to association |
| `domain_transfer_risk` | Urban cohort to older adults generally | Generalisation refused; scope qualifier added (arg-00000006) |
| `interpretive_plurality` | "Real but population-bounded" vs "no effect" | Both retained. Rival reading arg-00000009 preserved because the evidence does not distinguish them. |
| `weak_support` | Effect size reported without a confidence interval | Surfaced here rather than silently carried (finding rev-00000003) |
| `source_bias` | English-language, open-access-only retrieval | **Declared in the research plan before gathering**, and restated here |

## Declared coverage limits

Stated in the research plan before evidence gathering and unchanged by it:

* English-language sources only.
* Open-access sources only; subscription literature was not passage-verifiable.
* Two regions dominate the retrieved set (source diversity index 0.58, above the
  0.50 threshold but not high).

## What is NOT uncertain

The bounded association among adults aged 65-74 in the urban cohort is well
supported at high confidence with a verified passage-level citation. It is not in
question here.

## What would reduce this uncertainty

An adequately powered experimental or quasi-experimental design in a population
broader than a single urban cohort, with a validated measure of the named
construct, and non-English and subscription literature included in retrieval.
